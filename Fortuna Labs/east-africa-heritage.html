📜 About This Atlas

Why East Africa?

East Africa is one of the cradles of humankind — from the Australopithecine footprints of Laetoli (3.6 million years old) to the rock-hewn churches of Lalibela, the stone coral cities of the Swahili coast, and the irrigation terraces of the Meru and Chaga highlands. This atlas maps the region's heritage as one connected web rather than isolated national stories.

Built from Meru 🇰🇪

This edition is dedicated to Chris of Meru, Kenya — with Meru (0.35°N, 37.50°E) set as the atlas "home base". Every distance shown is a great-circle (Haversine) measurement from that point on the red soil beneath Kirinyaga, unless you enable your own location.

How to explore

Use the search box, category chips and country filter to narrow the atlas. Click a map marker to fly to its card. Enable geolocation to re-sort every site by real-world distance from wherever you stand. The sister project — the Nusantara Atlas — covers Indonesia and links back here.
